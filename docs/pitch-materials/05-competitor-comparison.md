## Talk track (one line per column)

- **ChatGPT/Claude:** great at generating text, but every task starts from a blank prompt and lives in one person's account. It answers; it doesn't *do* the browser work.
- **Zapier/Make:** solid for app-to-app triggers, but multi-step browser research is hard for a non-technical marketer to build and maintain.
- **Notion/SOPs:** documents describe the process — a human still executes every step by hand.
- **Hiring:** adds capacity but also salary, onboarding, management — and the process still lives in someone's head.
- **Twixy:** the only option that *executes* the work, is *reusable* across clients, is *shared* by the team, and is *visible* to the owner.

## Positioning sentence for the slide

> "The real competitor isn't another product — it's the founder's memory, a pile of tabs, and copy-paste. Twixy replaces the glue work, not your tools."

## Honesty guardrails (from feature-accuracy rules)

- Don't claim Twixy replaces project-management tools or content-scheduling tools — it sits alongside them.
- Execution logs: show what exists today; frame approval workflows and advanced permissions as planned.
- Automation reliability depends on the website and configuration — say so if asked, and pivot to the execution-log visibility story.
